Overview / Topics

One movement

Every change to a balance, on every token, goes through one internal function, _update. This page follows one movement through it: how its kind is chosen, what the rules are told, what the token checks itself, what each rule checks, and what the caller sees when the answer is no.

one path: _update in the base 5 kinds of movement up to 16 rules per token, first no wins 23 reason codes in Errors.sol

In one minute

Whatever the route, a transfer, a mint, a vault deposit or an enforcement action, the token ends up in _update. It labels the movement with a kind, writes down who is involved and their balances, and checks two things itself: whether it is paused, and whether the sender can move that much. Then it asks the hook, which asks the token's rules one by one. The first rule that answers anything other than TRANSFER_OK stops the movement, and the whole call reverts with that answer. If all agree, the balances move and rules that asked to be told afterwards are told. Read-only versions of the same path, the pre-flights, let anyone ask first.

The path of one movement

_update(from, to, amount) is the OpenZeppelin ERC-20 function that every balance change calls. The base overrides it once, and the two vaults pass straight through to that override, so every route reaches these steps in this order.

  • Step 1, the guard. _update is nonReentrant (OpenZeppelin's ReentrancyGuardTransient), so nothing called during the movement can start another movement on the same token.
  • Step 2, the kind. See Kinds. An announced kind is read from transient storage (storage that lasts one transaction) and cleared at once, so it cannot leak into the next movement.
  • Step 3, the context and the hook. See Context. The hook address is read once into a local variable, so the hook that approved the movement is the hook that hears about it afterwards.
  • Steps 4 and 5, the token's own checks. See The token's own checks.
  • Steps 6 and 7, the hook. An initialised token always has a hook: its initialiser and setPolicyHook refuse the zero address. See The rule chain.

The five kinds

The kind tells the rules whose authority moves the tokens. A rule can treat an enforcement move by the agent differently from a holder's transfer, even when the addresses are the same. Three kinds can be read from the addresses. The other two cannot, so the function that starts them announces them first, through _moveAs (between two accounts) or _mintAs (a mint). An announced kind wins over the addresses.

What the hook is told

The token builds a TransferContext for every movement, with one function, _context, for the write and for every pre-flight. Callers cannot supply one.

FieldValue
tokenThe token's own address. A shared hook and shared rules key everything by it.
fromThe sender; zero on a mint.
toThe receiver; zero on a burn.
idAlways 0 for these fungible tokens.
amountThe amount moved.
operatorThe account that called the token: the holder for transfer, the spender for transferFrom, the executor for executeTransfer, the minter, burner, agent or fulfiller for their calls, the caller of a vault function.
kindOne of the five kinds.
extraThe balances of from and to before the move, abi.encode(fromBalance, toBalance), each 0 for the zero address. For EXECUTED the intent's ref and deadline follow.

A rule reads the balances from extra and works out the balances after the move from amount, so on the movement path it makes no call back into the token for them. The only calls the deployed rules make to the token are views: hasRole, and policyHook in after_.

The token's own checks

Two checks are mechanical and live in the token, not in a rule. Both run before the hook is asked.

Pause

While the token is paused, every kind except FORCED is rejected with PolicyGated_TransferRejected(PAUSED): transfers, intents, mints and burns, vault deposits and redemptions. The guardian pauses with no delay. Only the main timelock (DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE) or the recovery timelock (RECOVERY_ROLE) can unpause.

What the sender can move

The agent can hold back part of any balance with setFrozenTokens(account, amount). The frozen amount may be larger than the balance. A holder can move only the unfrozen balance, balance - frozen, or nothing when the frozen amount is the larger. A FORCED move, and a BURN whose operator holds BURNER_ROLE, may take the whole balance, frozen tokens included, but never more.

CaseThe write reverts with
Amount within the balance but above what the sender can moveERC7943InsufficientUnfrozenBalance(account, amount, unfrozen), the ERC-7943 error
Amount above the whole balance, frozen or notERC20InsufficientBalance(account, balance, amount), the ERC-20 error
forcedTransfer or the burner's burn above the whole balanceERC20InsufficientBalance, from _releaseFrozen, before _update runs

Taking frozen tokens. forcedTransfer and the burner's burn first call _releaseFrozen, which lowers the frozen amount by exactly the part of the amount that the unfrozen balance does not cover, and emits Frozen before the Transfer. Example: a balance of 100 with 30 frozen. The holder can send 70. Sending 80 reverts with ERC7943InsufficientUnfrozenBalance (amount 80, unfrozen 70), and sending 120 with ERC20InsufficientBalance (balance 100, amount 120). A forced transfer of 80 takes the 70 unfrozen tokens and 10 frozen ones, and the frozen amount drops to 20. A vault redeem is a holder's burn and cannot take frozen tokens.

The rule chain

The hook, ComplianceHook, is shared by every token of a deployment. For each token it keeps an ordered list of rules, at most 16. beforeTransfer is a read-only call (view, made with STATICCALL), so neither the hook nor a rule can change anything while deciding.

These are the lists the deployment modules install (RULE_ORDER in ignition/modules/roles.ts). Each token passes its list to its initialiser, which installs it on the hook, and ProxyDeployer refuses to finish unless the hook holds exactly that list. Later, only the token's DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE holder (the main timelock) can replace a list (the token's own code calls setRules only in its initialiser), and only with rules attested as RULE, no two of the same type. The vaults have no executed intents, so they leave out the settlement window.

The FORCED column also covers the enforcement mint of recoverClaimable, where there is no sender. "Registered" means the token is a registered class of the fund and the rule is the fund's installed one; otherwise every movement gets NOT_FUND_CLASS.

CredentialRule

Asks CredentialRegistry.profileOf about each party. Sender first: an address that reads NONE or BLOCKED gets SENDER_NOT_VERIFIED, except in a burn whose operator holds BURNER_ROLE. Then the receiver: no policy for the token gives RECEIVER_NOT_ELIGIBLE, a lapsed credential CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED, any other standing but ACTIVE RECEIVER_NOT_VERIFIED, and an active credential the policy does not accept RECEIVER_NOT_ELIGIBLE. A holder whose credential lapsed can still send and redeem, and cannot receive.

MinPositionRule

Passes when the token's minimum is 0, the amount is 0, the sender is the receiver, or the kind is FORCED. Otherwise BELOW_MIN_POSITION when the receiver would end above zero and below the minimum, or the sender would keep a remainder above zero and below it. Exempt: the token itself (the 7540 escrow), accounts the token's admin lists, and a burner's burn on the sender side.

HolderRestrictionRule

For every kind but FORCED, a frozen sender gets SENDER_FROZEN (a burner's burn included), then a locked one gets LOCKED on a transfer, an intent or a holder's burn. A frozen receiver gets RECEIVER_FROZEN in every kind with a receiver, forced moves and mints included. The way out of a frozen address is a forced transfer to an unfrozen quarantine account.

SettlementWindowRule

Judges EXECUTED only. Outside the token's daily UTC window, or on a weekday the window does not include, it returns OUTSIDE_SETTLEMENT_WINDOW. An overnight window belongs to the day it opened.

FundHoldersRule

Returns NOT_FUND_CLASS for every movement of a token that is not a registered class of its fund, or when it is not the fund's installed rule. Otherwise a new investor (a receiver that holds no class of the fund, is not exempt and is not the class itself) passes while the count is below the cap, or at the cap when the sender empties its only class. Anything else gets MAX_HOLDERS. Zero amounts, burns, forced moves and receivers who already hold a class are never capped.

After the balances move

Once the balances have moved, the token asks the hook wantsAfter(). ComplianceHook answers yes when at least one rule in the calling token's list asked for after_ when the list was installed. Then the token calls afterTransfer(ctx), which the hook accepts only from the token named in the context (ComplianceHook_OnlyToken), and the hook calls after_ on those rules. Each rule accepts it only from the hook that token currently has installed (ComplianceRule_OnlyHook).

FundHoldersRule is the one deployed rule with an after_. It runs for every kind. It does nothing for a zero amount or a move to oneself. Otherwise it calls Fund.join(token, to) when the receiver held none of this class before the move, and Fund.leave(token, from) when the move takes the sender's whole balance. The fund keeps the investor count; it never counts the class token itself.

This step can write state, so it runs while the token's reentrancy guard is still held: a rule that calls back into any function that moves balances reverts with ReentrancyGuardReentrantCall. If after_ reverts, the whole movement reverts.

Asking first: the pre-flights

canTransferWithReason, canTransfer and canExecute run the same _check as the write, without writing, so a wallet or an integrator can learn the answer before sending; canSend and canReceive ask the hook's account questions instead. They answer for the block they read; a transaction mined later sees a later time, which matters at a lockup end, a window edge or an intent deadline.

Where they differ from the write

  • canTransfer does not fail on a plain balance shortfall. ERC-7943 forbids it. With nothing frozen on the sender, an amount above the balance reads true when the hook accepts it, while canTransferWithReason says INSUFFICIENT_UNFROZEN and the write reverts with ERC20InsufficientBalance. Once part of the balance is frozen, an amount above the unfrozen balance reads false in both, except a burn by a sender that holds BURNER_ROLE. To learn whether a movement will succeed, call canTransferWithReason or canExecute.
  • They model a holder-driven movement. canTransfer and canTransferWithReason cannot ask about FORCED or EXECUTED. A burn is asked as the sender's own burn, so frozen tokens and the lockup count, unless the sender itself holds BURNER_ROLE.
  • canExecute does not check EXECUTOR_ROLE, and it compares the intent's executor with its own caller. Call it from the executor's address.
  • The account questions leave out amounts and counts. canSend and canReceive do not see the minimum position or the fund's investor cap: a new investor at a full fund reads true in canReceive, and the movement is refused with MAX_HOLDERS. They also cannot see the kinds a rule exempts: canSend is false for a locked holder although a forced transfer from it still passes.
  • None of them runs afterTransfer.

The vaults' maxDeposit and maxMint pre-flight a mint of type(uint128).max shares, large enough that a minimum position does not read as a block and not zero, so the fund's cap sees a new investor. maxRedeem and maxWithdraw pre-flight a burn of nothing from the owner. On Gated7540Vault the same probes run on the controller, and also guard requestDeposit and the claims. Where the money sits and the vault pages follow those flows.

Why it is built this way

  • One path, no exceptions. Every entry point ends in _update, so a new entry point cannot forget a check, and the hook sees mints and burns as movements to and from the zero address.
  • The base holds no policy. Pause and the frozen amount are mechanics every token needs. Everything about who, when and how much is a rule, so it can change without an upgrade.
  • One check for the write and the pre-flights. _check and _context serve both, so a pre-flight sees what the write would, apart from the differences listed above.
  • Typed errors, never a silent false. A rejection reverts and carries its reason, and a balance problem carries the standard error that names the cause.
  • Rules answer, they do not revert. check is read-only and returns a code, so a pre-flight can report the reason instead of failing.
