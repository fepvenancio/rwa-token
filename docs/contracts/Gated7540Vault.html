Contracts / Vaults

Gated7540Vault

An ERC-7540 vault, where nothing settles at once. An investor files a request, the bank settles it later at a price it names, and the investor then claims the result. The shares are a gated token. The vault checks the account a request is booked under when the request is filed and again when it is claimed, and the mint itself judges the account that receives the shares.

contracts/vaults/Gated7540Vault.sol upgradeable: UUPS behind a TokenProxy admin: the main timelock implementation attested as IMPLEMENTATION

In one minute

Many funds cannot settle a subscription the moment it arrives, because the price is worked out later, off chain. ERC-7540 is the standard vault for that: the investor files a request, the fund settles it, and the investor then claims the result. Here the settling is done by an account with FULFILLER_ROLE, the bank, which names both the assets and the shares of each settlement. The contract computes no price of its own.

Deposited assets wait in the vault until the settlement sends them to a custody address. Shares being redeemed wait in the vault too, in escrow (held on the investor's behalf until the request is settled), and the settlement burns them. The shares are a gated token, so each step that moves shares passes the compliance checks, and the vault checks the investor again at the claim, because a lot can change between a request and its settlement. When an investor is not allowed to claim, an agent can deliver what is owed to a verified account.

How it works

Every request goes through three steps, and each step is a separate transaction: the owner requests, the fulfiller settles, the controller claims. Three names matter. The owner is the account whose assets or shares a request takes. The controller is the account the request is booked under: it claims the result, and the eligibility checks are about it. The receiver is picked at the claim and gets the shares or the assets.

Requests add up per controller. The request id is always 0, and for each side the vault keeps one pending figure and one claimable pair per controller. A controller can have pending and claimable amounts at the same time, for example after a partial fulfilment.

Deposits

  1. Request. requestDeposit(assets, controller, owner), called by the owner or an operator of the owner. The checks run in this order: assets not zero (Gated7540Vault_ZeroAmount), controller not zero (Gated7540Vault_ZeroAddress), the caller allowed (Gated7540Vault_Unauthorized(caller)), then the mint probe on the controller (Gated7540Vault_ControllerNotEligible(reason)). The assets come from the owner, which must have approved the vault on the asset. The owner's own credential is not checked. Emits DepositRequest.
  2. Fulfil. fulfillDeposit(controller, assets, shares), called by FULFILLER_ROLE. The fulfiller names how many pending assets it settles and how many shares they buy, and it can settle a request in parts. Both numbers must be non-zero (Gated7540Vault_ZeroAmount), something must be pending (Gated7540Vault_NothingPending), and no more than is pending (Gated7540Vault_ExceedsPending(requested, pending)). The vault records the price, emits Fulfilled, and sends the assets to custody. No shares move, so the hook is not called.
  3. Claim. deposit(assets, receiver, controller) or mint(shares, receiver, controller), called by the controller or its operator. The two-argument deposit(assets, receiver) and mint(shares, receiver) claim for the caller as controller. The controller must pass the mint probe again. The caller picks the receiver, and the shares are minted to it through the hook, so the receiver is checked too, including any minimum position. Emits Deposit(controller, receiver, assets, shares).

Redemptions

  1. Request. requestRedeem(shares, controller, owner), called by the owner, an operator of the owner, or any account with the owner's share allowance, which it spends. Shares and controller must be non-zero. The shares move from the owner into the vault as an ordinary TRANSFER through the hook, so pause, the owner's frozen amount, credential, lockup, address freeze and minimum position all apply, and the vault itself must be able to receive. The controller is not checked at this step. Emits RedeemRequest.
  2. Fulfil. fulfillRedeem(controller, shares, assets), called by FULFILLER_ROLE, with the same three checks as a deposit fulfilment. The escrowed shares are burned as a BURN from the vault through the hook, then the vault pulls assets from the fulfiller's own wallet, which must have approved it. The burn is judged against the vault, not the controller.
  3. Claim. redeem(shares, receiver, controller) or withdraw(assets, receiver, controller), called by the controller or its operator. These are the ERC-4626 signatures with the third argument as the controller. They refuse while paused (EnforcedPause), then the controller must pass the payout probe. The assets go to the receiver the caller picks, as a plain transfer. Emits Withdraw(caller, receiver, controller, assets, shares).

Controllers and operators

An operator is an account that a controller or owner approved with setOperator(operator, approved), which sets or clears the approval for the caller and emits OperatorSet(controller, operator, approved). The same approval counts in two places.

The approval is given byThe operator can
an ownerCall requestDeposit and requestRedeem for it. A deposit request still needs the owner's asset allowance for the vault. A redemption request needs no share allowance.
a controllerClaim for it with deposit, mint, redeem and withdraw, and choose the receiver.

Operators are full delegates, as ERC-7540 defines them. An operator of an owner can open a request under any controller, its own included, that takes the owner's assets or shares. A test pins this, so that nobody narrows it by accident. An account should approve only operators it would trust with its assets and shares. An account that is neither the owner nor its operator gets Gated7540Vault_Unauthorized(caller) on a deposit request, and needs a share allowance for a redemption request, as with an ERC-20 transferFrom.

Eligibility at the request and at the claim

The vault checks the controller, the account a request is booked under, when the request is filed and again at the claim. At a deposit claim the mint then judges the receiver as well. It uses two pre-flights, which run the same path as a real movement without the write. The mint probe asks whether the hook would accept a mint of type(uint128).max shares to the controller. The payout probe asks whether it would accept a burn of 0 shares from the controller.

  • The controller, not the owner. The controller is the account the request is booked under and the one that claims. The vault probes it at the request and at the claim, and the real mint then judges the receiver the claim names. A verified owner cannot open a deposit request for a controller that is not verified, and a claim is refused for an ineligible controller even when it names an eligible receiver. Tests pin both.
  • Checked again at the claim. A fulfilment can come long after the request. If the controller's credential lapses or is blocked, or the fund fills up, in between, a deposit claim reverts. A redemption claim reverts only when the controller has no credential or is blocked, address-frozen or locked. Either way the amounts stay claimable until they can be claimed or the agent recovers them.
  • Lapsed and blocked are different. With the real CredentialRule, a controller whose credential lapsed can still request a redemption and collect its payout, but cannot request or claim a deposit (CREDENTIAL_EXPIRED). A blocked controller cannot collect a payout (SENDER_NOT_VERIFIED).
  • Why the payout probe burns nothing. The controller's shares went into escrow and were burned at the fulfilment, so it usually holds none. A burn of 0 asks who the controller is without the frozen-balance check.
  • The minimum position is judged at the claim. A request cannot know how many shares it will buy, and the large probe amount is above any minimum, so a fresh controller is not refused for size. If the shares claimed, added to what the receiver already holds, come to less than the minimum, the claim reverts with PolicyGated_TransferRejected(BELOW_MIN_POSITION) and consumes nothing.
  • max* follow the probes. maxDeposit and maxMint are the controller's claimable assets and shares, or 0 if the mint probe fails. maxRedeem and maxWithdraw are its claimable shares and assets, or 0 if the payout probe fails. A pause makes all four 0.

Price per fulfilment

The fulfiller names both numbers of each fulfilment: how many pending assets and how many shares they buy, or how many pending shares and how many assets they pay. The vault compares that price with nothing on chain. The controller's protection is that FULFILLER_ROLE is a trusted role.

  • Each fulfilment records a price. lastPrice = floor(assets × 10^decimals / shares), the assets for one whole share, raised to 1 if it rounds down to 0. It is emitted in Fulfilled(controller, isDeposit, price, assets, shares, fulfiller), so the events are the fund's price history on chain, and a test rebuilds that history from them. Before the first fulfilment lastPrice is 10^decimals, one asset per share.
  • The floor of 1 keeps convertToShares, which divides by lastPrice, from reverting after a fulfilment priced below one unit per whole share.
  • Indicative views. convertToShares(a) = floor(a × 10^decimals / lastPrice) and convertToAssets(s) = floor(s × lastPrice / 10^decimals). totalAssets() is the share supply valued at lastPrice, rounded down. It is not what the vault holds: the invested assets are in custody.
  • Claims never use lastPrice. Each fulfilment adds its assets and shares to the controller's claimable pair, and a claim uses the ratio of that pair. Two fulfilments at different prices blend in it.
  • No decimals offset. Shares have the asset's decimals. The vault computes no exchange rate from its balances, so there is nothing for a donation to push.

Rounding of partial claims

A claim can take part of what is claimable. Each partial claim rounds in the vault's favour, the difference stays in the claimable pair, and a claim of everything clears the pair exactly, so over all its claims a controller receives exactly the fulfilled amounts.

ClaimPartial claimClaim of everything
deposit(assets, …)shares = floor(assets × claimableShares / claimableAssets); 0 shares reverts ZeroAmountall claimable shares
mint(shares, …)assets used = ceil(shares × claimableAssets / claimableShares)all claimable assets
redeem(shares, …)assets = floor(shares × claimableAssets / claimableShares); 0 assets reverts ZeroAmountall claimable assets
withdraw(assets, …)shares used = ceil(assets × claimableShares / claimableAssets)all claimable shares

Example in raw units: with 1,000 assets and 800 shares claimable, deposit(333) mints floor(333 × 800 / 1000) = 266 shares and leaves 667 and 534. mint(267) then uses ceil(267 × 667 / 534) = 334 assets. Because mint and withdraw round up, they can use one side of the pair up first. The rest of the other side is then delivered against zero, which the code allows on purpose: it is owed either way. A zero input, or a claim above what is claimable (Gated7540Vault_ExceedsClaimable(requested, claimable)), reverts on all four.

Reject and recover

A request does not have to end in a claim. The fulfiller can reject what is pending, and the agent (AGENT_ROLE, the enforcement role) can deliver what a controller cannot take itself to a verified account. There is no cancel for the investor.

  • A reject is an ordinary transfer. Returning shares to the controller runs the whole policy: pause, a freeze on the escrow, the controller's credential, address freeze and minimum, and the fund cap if the controller would be a new investor. So naming a new investor as controller and having the request rejected fails the same way a direct transfer would. A test pins this.
  • When a reject cannot go through, it reverts and the shares stay in escrow. The agent's recoverRedeemRequest then moves them to a verified account. The fund cap does not stop it, and the fund still counts the new investor.
  • recoverClaimable covers what is already fulfilled: a controller that became ineligible after its fulfilment, or a deposit stranded because the fund filled up before the claim. The deposit's assets are already in custody, so it delivers the shares they bought. It works at a full cap, and while paused.
  • A frozen escrow. The agent can freeze part of the vault's own balance with setFrozenTokens. A reject or a fulfilment that needs more than the escrow's unfrozen balance is then refused with ERC7943InsufficientUnfrozenBalance, while recoverRedeemRequest still moves the shares and lowers the frozen amount by the part it takes, as forcedTransfer does.

Custody and the escrow

Custody is a plain address. It is set when the vault is initialised, a zero address is refused, and the main timelock changes it with setCustody, which emits CustodyChanged(old, new). Fulfilled deposit assets go there. The vault never pulls from it: redemption payouts come from the fulfiller's own wallet at the fulfilment. The code makes no assumption about what the custody address is.

The escrow is the vault's own share balance: the shares of pending redemptions. Two invariant tests hold it to the bookkeeping. The vault's share balance always equals the sum of pending redemptions, and its asset balance always equals the pending deposit assets plus the unclaimed redemption payouts.

The escrow account is the token itself. FundHoldersRule never counts it as an investor and MinPositionRule exempts it, so an escrow below the minimum is fine. An owner who escrows its whole balance stops holding this class at once, and if it holds no other class of the fund, the fund's investor count drops at the request, before any fulfilment. CredentialRule makes no such exception: the vault receives the escrow and sends it back, so it needs its own credential that the vault's policy accepts. The KYC attester issues it after deployment, a step the deployment module leaves to the attester.

Why it is built this way

  • The request is the instruction. There are no signed intents on this vault. An investor's request is recorded on chain, and only FULFILLER_ROLE settles it, at the amounts it names. That fits a fund whose price is set off chain.
  • No cancel, so a check at the door. The investor cannot take a deposit request back, so the vault refuses a request whose controller could not hold the shares at all, rather than take assets it could never turn into shares. The fulfiller can still reject.
  • Compliance holds after the fulfilment. Claims re-run the controller's pre-flight, and the agent's recoveries give a way out for amounts the controller cannot claim.
  • Rejects follow the policy, recoveries are enforcement. A reject is an ordinary transfer, so it cannot carry shares past a pause or a full fund. The recoveries are forced moves for the agent, and the account they deliver to must still be verified.
  • Prices are explicit. The fulfiller states both sides of each fulfilment and the vault keeps the price in an event. With no on-chain exchange rate, the shares need no decimals offset, and claims use each controller's own claimable ratio.
  • Exact totals. Partial claims round in the vault's favour and a claim of everything clears the pair exactly, so nothing is lost or created across claims.
  • The same gated token. Shares are the base token: the escrow transfer, the burn and the claim mint all go through the hook. The issuer mint and burn revert, so the share supply moves only through claims, the agent's recoverClaimable and fulfilments.
  • Reviewed code only. The vault is a proxy: a small contract that keeps the address and the balances and runs shared code, its implementation. An upgrade must target an implementation that the PolicyRegistry attests, meaning the security council reviewed its code and recorded its code hash, and that reports the template id keccak256("Gated7540Vault"). Tests show the vault refusing attested IntentToken and Gated4626Vault implementations with Templated_WrongTemplate; an implementation that is not attested is refused before that, with PolicyGated_NotAttested. There is no receive function. The only payable function is the inherited upgradeToAndCall, which only UPGRADER_ROLE can call and which refuses ETH when the upgrade carries no call.
