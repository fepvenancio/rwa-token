Contracts / Settlement

DvPExecutor

Settles a trade of a security token against cash in one transaction: the shares move and the cash moves, or nothing moves. Each side signs what it gives and what it is to receive, and the executor checks that the two signed statements describe the same trade.

contracts/intents/DvPExecutor.sol immutable, not a proxy admin: the main timelock not attested: a token trusts it by granting EXECUTOR_ROLE

In one minute

Delivery versus payment (DvP) means the seller's shares are delivered only if the buyer pays, and the buyer pays only if the shares are delivered. The two parties agree a trade off-chain. The seller signs an intent (a signed transfer instruction) to send shares to the buyer, stating the cash token and the price it expects in return. The buyer signs the payment in one of two ways: an intent on a gated cash token, stating the shares it expects, or an ERC-3009 authorization on a USDC-style token, tied to the seller's intent. An operator submits both. The executor checks that they match, runs the cash first, then the shares, and if any part fails the whole transaction is undone. It does no matching, pricing, fees or partial fills.

How a trade settles

Two entry points, one per form of cash. Both are called by an OPERATOR_ROLE holder, both are guarded against reentry, and both stop while the executor is paused. Neither can run one leg alone.

What each party signs

Each signature covers both what its signer gives and what it is to receive. The executor builds nothing itself; it only compares the two signed statements.

In the ERC-3009 form the buyer signs instead an authorization under the cash token's own rules: from the buyer, payable to the executor, for the price, with a validity window and the nonce described below. The executor passes itself as the payee; ERC-3009's receiveWithAuthorization can only be submitted by its payee.

An intent that states a consideration must name its executor, and the token refuses it from any other caller. So both intents of a trade name this executor, and neither can be run by the IntentExecutor or anyone else. The executor does not compare the executor fields itself; each token does.

When the two legs describe one trade

Gated form: the legs mirror

  • The two tokens differ.
  • The parties are swapped: the seller's from is the buyer's to, and the seller's to is the buyer's from.
  • Each amount is the other leg's stated consideration: the shares equal the buyer's considerationAmount, the price equals the seller's.
  • Each stated consideration token is the other leg's token.

ERC-3009 form: the authorization matches

  • The two tokens differ.
  • The authorization is from the seller's to, the buyer.
  • Its value is the seller's considerationAmount, and the seller's considerationToken is the cash token.
  • Its nonce is derived from the security token and the seller's intent, as below.

Any mismatch reverts with DvPExecutor_LegsDoNotMatch before any cash moves. In the gated form no token has been called yet; in the ERC-3009 form only the security token's intentHash has been read.

The ERC-3009 nonce

The buyer's authorization carries this nonce:

keccak256(abi.encode(securityToken, securityToken.intentHash(securitiesIntent)))

The intent hash ties the payment to one exact securities intent: same seller, buyer, shares, price, deadline and nonce. The security token's address is in it on purpose. The executor cannot tell an honest token's intentHash from a counterfeit's, because it simply asks the token. With the bare hash, an operator could pair the buyer's authorization with a counterfeit "security token" that reports the genuine hash, delivers nothing, and names the operator's own account as the seller, so the cash would go to the operator. With the token's address inside, that authorization only matches the token the buyer meant. A test tries exactly this substitution and expects DvPExecutor_LegsDoNotMatch with no cash moved.

The buyer's wallet shows the value, the executor as payee and an opaque nonce, so in this form it cannot show what the buyer receives. In the gated form the buyer's intent states the shares in clear.

Cash conservation in the ERC-3009 form

The executor never keeps settlement money. Before the pull it records its own cash balance. After paying the seller it requires two things, and reverts with DvPExecutor_CashNotConserved(account, expected, actual) otherwise:

  • its own balance equals the balance it recorded before the pull;
  • the seller's balance rose by exactly the price. The seller's balance is read after the pull and before the payout, so a seller who is also the payer is measured on the payout alone.

A cash token that takes a fee on transfers fails the trade. Usually the payout itself reverts with the cash token's own error, because the executor received less than it must pay. When a stray balance in the executor covers the shortfall, the conservation check catches it. Anything sent to the executor by mistake stays there: it has no function to sweep it out.

Errors, and what is not wrapped

When a leg fails, the executor wraps the token's revert data so the operator knows which leg failed. The cash leg runs first, so if both legs would fail, the cash leg is the one reported.

ErrorWhen
DvPExecutor_LegsDoNotMatch()The legs do not describe one trade (above).
DvPExecutor_LegFailed(leg, reason)A token refused a leg. leg is CASH (0) or SECURITIES (1); reason is the token's revert data, for example PolicyGated_TransferRejected(reason). In the ERC-3009 form the cash leg is the pull.
DvPExecutor_CashNotConserved(account, expected, actual)ERC-3009 form only: the executor or the seller does not end with the required balance.

Not wrapped, so they arrive with their own error:

  • The payout to the seller in the ERC-3009 form: the cash token's own error (for a blocklisted seller, say), SafeERC20FailedOperation(token) for a token that returns false, and ReentrancyGuardReentrantCall for a token that calls back into the executor.
  • The executor's own gates: AccessControlUnauthorizedAccount, EnforcedPause, ReentrancyGuardReentrantCall.
  • A leg whose token address has no code reverts with empty revert data, because Solidity does not route that failure to the catch.

Pre-flights, and what they cannot see

canExecuteDvPGated and canExecuteDvP3009 return (ok, reason) and never revert on a refusal. They ask the tokens' own canExecute from the executor's address, so a token's executor binding answers as it will in the real call. In order:

  1. The executor's pause: PAUSED.
  2. The mirror: LEGS_DO_NOT_MATCH.
  3. The cash token's canExecute for the buyer's intent.
  4. The security token's canExecute for the seller's intent.
  1. The executor's pause: PAUSED.
  2. The match: LEGS_DO_NOT_MATCH.
  3. The authorization: INTENT_NOT_YET_VALID at or before validAfter, INTENT_EXPIRED at or after validBefore, NONCE_USED if used or cancelled, INSUFFICIENT_UNFROZEN if the buyer's balance is below the value.
  4. The security token's canExecute for the seller's intent.

What they cannot see:

  • Roles. Neither checks OPERATOR_ROLE, nor that the executor still holds EXECUTOR_ROLE on the tokens. After a token revokes the executor, the pre-flight says yes and the trade fails.
  • The ERC-3009 side. The buyer's signature (the cash token exposes no view for it), a pause or blocklist on the cash token, and a transfer fee.
  • One leg's effect on the other. Both legs are asked against the state before either runs. When the two tokens share policy state, such as two classes of one Fund, the first leg can change the second's answer. A test shows it: a fund whose investor cap has been lowered below its current count, and a buyer who swaps its only class for another. The buyer leaves the fund on the first leg and counts as a new investor on the second, which the lowered cap refuses, though the pre-flight says yes.

Simulating the execute call itself with eth_call from the operator's address shows the real outcome.

Time, cancellation and policy

  • Deadlines differ at the edge. An intent's deadline is inclusive: it is valid in the deadline's second. An ERC-3009 validBefore is exclusive. A buyer who signs validBefore equal to the intent's deadline loses that last second, and a trade submitted in it reverts as a whole; one second later, both are valid on the deadline.
  • Cancelling. The seller, and the buyer in the gated form, cancel by spending their nonce with cancelIntent on their own token. An ERC-3009 authorization is cancelled on the cash token by whatever means it offers; the pre-flight then reads NONCE_USED. Either way the trade then fails as a whole.
  • Expiry. A trade nobody submits lapses at the intent's deadline or the authorization's validBefore.
  • Policy applies to each executed leg. The securities leg reaches the hook as EXECUTED, so the settlement window, credentials, holder restrictions, minimum position and the fund-wide holder cap apply to it unchanged. A gated cash leg runs the cash token's own rule list, pause and frozen amounts. The shares go straight from the seller to the buyer, so the executor never holds them and needs no credential.

Roles, pause and reentry

OPERATOR_ROLE
Submits trades. Deployed: the operator account, the same as on the intent executor.
GUARDIAN_ROLE
Pauses with no delay. Deployed: the guardian multisig.
DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE
Grants roles and unpauses. Deployed: the main timelock.
RECOVERY_ROLE
Only unpause() and revoking GUARDIAN_ROLE. Deployed: the recovery timelock.
EXECUTOR_ROLE
Held by this contract on each token it settles. The deployment grants it on the intent token. A gated cash token is a separate instrument; its own administrator grants the role.

Pausing the executor stops both entry points without touching the tokens. Both entry points carry OpenZeppelin's transient reentrancy guard, so a cash token that calls back into the executor during the pull, the payout or a gated leg is stopped; the tests cover all three. The pre-flights are read-only and unguarded.

Why it is built this way

  • Both legs or neither. There is no entry point that runs one leg alone, and an intent that states a payment can only be run by the executor it names. The IntentExecutor refuses such intents outright.
  • The operator cannot redirect value. It chooses which signed legs to pair, when to submit and which token addresses to pass. But in the gated form each signature covers the other party, both amounts and both tokens, and the mirror makes them agree; in the ERC-3009 form the nonce commits to the whole securities intent and the security token's address, and the payout goes to the intent's from. Each signature is valid only on its own token's domain. There is no on-chain list of tokens, so a party who signs a trade naming a counterfeit token loses to it.
  • Cash first. The legs revert together, so the order decides which failure is reported: a trade where both would fail reports the cash leg. In the ERC-3009 form the executor must hold the cash before it can pay the seller.
  • The tokens decide. Each token stays the single source of truth for signatures, nonces, time windows, executor binding and policy. The executor adds only the check that the legs describe one trade, atomicity, and in the ERC-3009 form the balance checks.
  • No state, replaceable. It stores its roles and pause flag only, and never keeps settlement funds. It is a plain contract; replacing it means deploying a new one and moving the tokens' EXECUTOR_ROLE.
