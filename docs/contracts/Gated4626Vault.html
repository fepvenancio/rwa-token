Contracts / Vaults

Gated4626Vault

A standard ERC-4626 vault. An investor pays in an asset and gets shares in the same transaction, and hands shares back for the asset the same way. The shares are a gated token, so only accounts the policy accepts can receive them, and the vault tells you in advance who would be refused.

contracts/vaults/Gated4626Vault.sol upgradeable: UUPS behind a TokenProxy admin: the main timelock implementation attested as IMPLEMENTATION

In one minute

A vault is a shared pot. You put an asset in, for example a stablecoin, and receive shares that stand for your part of the pot. You hand the shares back and take your part of the asset out. ERC-4626 is the common standard for such vaults, so wallets and other contracts already know how to use this one.

What is different here is the shares. They are a gated token: creating, moving and destroying them goes through the same compliance checks as every other token in the system. An account without an accepted credential cannot receive shares, and a holder who is locked up, frozen or blocked cannot redeem. The vault reports this up front: maxDeposit and maxRedeem return 0 for an account the policy would refuse. The asset itself moves as a plain ERC-20 transfer and is not checked.

How it works

Deposits and redemptions are OpenZeppelin's ERC4626Upgradeable (version 5.6.1), unchanged. The vault adds two things. Every change to a share balance runs through the base token's single _update path, which asks the policy hook first. And the four max* functions ask the hook too.

The hook is the shared ComplianceHook. It runs the vault's ordered list of rules (small contracts that each check one thing, such as credentials or lockups) and answers TRANSFER_OK or a reason code. A deposit reaches it as a MINT to the receiver, a redemption as a BURN from the owner, and in both the caller is the operator, the account driving the movement.

  1. The max check comes first. deposit refuses an amount above maxDeposit(receiver) with ERC4626ExceededMaxDeposit(receiver, assets, max). mint, withdraw and redeem do the same with maxMint, maxWithdraw and maxRedeem and their own ERC4626ExceededMax… errors. For an account the policy refuses, the max is 0, so any amount above zero stops here.
  2. Who pays and who receives. On a deposit the caller pays the asset (it must have approved the vault on the asset) and receiver gets the shares. On a redemption the shares come from owner, a caller that is not the owner spends the owner's share allowance, and receiver gets the asset.
  3. The hook decides on the shares. The base token builds the movement's context (kind, sender, receiver, amount, operator and both balances before the move) and calls beforeTransfer. A rule's reason code reverts the call with PolicyGated_TransferRejected(reason). This is where a check that depends on the amount shows up, such as a minimum position (BELOW_MIN_POSITION), which the max functions do not see.
  4. After the move. If a rule asks for it, the hook's afterTransfer runs once the balances have changed. FundHoldersRule uses it to keep the fund's investor count.

The max functions: refusals you can see in advance

ERC-4626 lets a vault say how much an account may deposit or redeem right now. Integrators are expected to read these numbers before they call, and the vault's own deposit and redeem functions check them too. Here they carry the policy. Each one runs a pre-flight, the same path a real movement takes without the write, and returns 0 if it would be refused.

FunctionReturns
maxDeposit(receiver), maxMint(receiver)0 if the vault is paused or the hook would refuse a mint of type(uint128).max shares to receiver. Otherwise type(uint256).max.
maxRedeem(owner)0 if the vault is paused or the hook would refuse a burn of 0 shares from owner. Otherwise the unfrozen balance, balanceOf(owner) − getFrozenTokens(owner), or 0 when the frozen amount is the larger.
maxWithdraw(owner)maxRedeem(owner) converted to assets, rounded down.

Why those amounts

  • A very large mint, not one share. The amount is not zero, so the fund's investor cap sees a new investor and refuses one when the fund is full. And it is above any minimum position up to type(uint128).max, so a minimum does not make an empty account look blocked, as a one-unit probe would.
  • A burn of nothing. Burning one share would leave a remainder, which a minimum position rejects, and would hide a holder who can redeem everything. The size of a real redemption is judged when it burns.
  • Rounding down for maxWithdraw. withdraw rounds the shares it burns up. Deriving maxWithdraw from maxRedeem with floor rounding means the vault never promises assets that withdraw could not deliver. A fuzz test moves the share price and checks that withdraw(maxWithdraw(owner)) always succeeds.
  • The limit of the large probe. A rule that capped the size of a position, such as a maximum balance or a supply cap, would refuse the probe and turn maxDeposit and maxMint to 0. None of the deployed rules does that.
  • Where maxRedeem can overstate. With a minimum position set, if a frozen amount below the minimum would stay behind, redeem(maxRedeem(owner)) reverts with PolicyGated_TransferRejected(BELOW_MIN_POSITION): the minimum judges the whole balance, maxRedeem only the unfrozen part. Redeeming less, so that at least the minimum stays, works. A test pins this case.
  • Whose call it is. The mint probe treats the caller of maxDeposit as the operator, and the burn probe treats the owner as the operator, while a real redemption's operator is its caller. Only a BURNER_ROLE holder could make the two differ, and the deployment grants that role on no vault.

Price, previews and the decimals offset

The vault holds the asset itself. totalAssets() is its balance of the asset, and the share price follows from OpenZeppelin's formulas, which add 106 virtual shares and one virtual unit of the asset that nobody owns:

shares = assets × (totalSupply + 10^6) / (totalAssets + 1)
assets = shares × (totalAssets + 1) / (totalSupply + 10^6)
CallConvertsRounds
deposit, previewDepositassets to sharesdown: the receiver gets slightly fewer shares
mint, previewMintshares to assetsup: the caller pays slightly more
withdraw, previewWithdrawassets to sharesup: slightly more shares are burned
redeem, previewRedeemshares to assetsdown: slightly fewer assets are paid

The previews are arithmetic only. They tell you what the formula gives, and the max functions tell you what the policy allows. The price moves only with the vault's asset balance and share supply: deposits, redemptions, their rounding, and any asset sent straight to the vault. There is no fee, yield or strategy code.

Why an offset of 6. _decimalsOffset() returns 6, which gives the formula its 106 virtual shares. Without them, the first depositor could deposit one unit, then send assets straight to the vault so that one share is worth so much that the next deposit rounds down to zero shares and its assets fall to the existing shares. The virtual shares make that attack cost the attacker more than it gains. A fuzz test runs it: the attacker never gets back more than it put in, and the victim loses at most about the donation divided by 106.

Shares have 6 more decimals than the asset. With a 6-decimal asset, shares have 12. A first deposit of 1,000 asset units (1000e6) into an empty vault mints 1e15 raw shares, which is 1,000 shares, and redeeming them returns exactly 1000e6. A minimum position is set in shares, so it counts in those 12 decimals.

Why it is built this way

  • OpenZeppelin's vault, unchanged. Deposits, redemptions, rounding and events come from ERC4626Upgradeable. The vault's own code is initialize, the max* functions, the decimals offset, the reverting issuer functions, supportsInterface (which adds ERC-4626), templateId, and _update, which both parents define and which the vault resolves explicitly to the base token's version, so every share movement takes the hooked path.
  • The policy shows through max*. ERC-4626 expresses limits through these functions, and integrators read them before calling. Putting the policy there means a blocked account sees 0 instead of an unexplained revert, and the vault's own functions enforce the same answer.
  • Shares only through deposits and redemptions. The base token's issuer mint and burn revert, so the share supply cannot grow without assets coming in. An invariant test checks that the whole share supply converts to no more assets than the vault holds.
  • The asset leg is not hooked. The policy is about who holds shares. The asset is an ordinary ERC-20 and moves with OpenZeppelin's SafeERC20 calls.
  • Reviewed code only. The vault is a proxy: a small contract that keeps the address and the balances and runs shared code, its implementation. An upgrade (UPGRADER_ROLE, which the deployment gives to the main timelock) must target an implementation that the PolicyRegistry attests, meaning the security council reviewed its code and recorded its code hash, and that reports the same template id, keccak256("Gated4626Vault"). Tests show the vault refusing attested IntentToken and Gated7540Vault implementations with Templated_WrongTemplate. An implementation that is not attested is refused before that, with PolicyGated_NotAttested.
  • No ETH. There is no receive function. The only payable function is the inherited upgradeToAndCall, which only UPGRADER_ROLE can call and which refuses ETH when the upgrade carries no call. Tests show a plain ETH send reverting.
