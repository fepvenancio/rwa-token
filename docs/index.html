Overview

Gated Token Stack

Tokens for fund shares and other real-world assets, where a policy decides who may hold them. Each token keeps its own books and its emergency powers, and asks a separate, replaceable set of compliance rules before a balance changes.

40 contracts in contracts/ (interfaces and test mocks aside) Solidity 0.8.30, OpenZeppelin 5.6.1 ERC-20, ERC-721, ERC-1155, ERC-7943, ERC-8473, ERC-4626, ERC-7540 licence: UNLICENSED, proprietary no external audit yet

In one minute

An issuer of fund shares usually may sell only to approved investors, and must be able to act on an account: pause the token during an incident, hold back part of a balance, or move tokens out of an account. A plain ERC-20 token lets anyone send to anyone and has none of these powers. Here every token keeps the books and the powers, and hands each question about who may hold, send or receive to one outside contract, the hook. The hook asks a short list of small contracts, the rules, and the first rule that says no stops the movement. Rules can be replaced through governance without touching the token or its address.

Around that core sit optional parts: executors that run signed transfers, vaults that deal at once or at an approved price, a portfolio that limits where a vault's money may go, and a contract that pays distributions. An issuer deploys one core and adds products one at a time. Nothing here has had an external audit.

The problem it solves

Real-world assets on a chain need things a plain token does not give. Who may hold them is decided by someone outside the chain: an identity provider, the issuer's onboarding. The issuer needs enforcement powers. The policy changes over time: a type of investor is added, a lockup starts, a fund limits its number of investors. And a fund needs a price someone has approved, and limits on where its money may go.

The stack answers with a split. The token is a standard ERC-20 that refuses to move a balance unless its hook says yes. The hook is shared by every token of a deployment and keeps an ordered list of rules for each token. Each rule answers one question, such as "does the receiver hold a credential this token accepts" or "would this leave a balance below the minimum". Every token also implements ERC-7943, a standard interface for permissioned tokens, so outside tools can ask it canTransfer, canSend and canReceive before they send. Every token and the hook also implement ERC-8473, a draft standard that sits behind ERC-7943 and names the policy contract a token asks.

Everything else is a separate contract a product opts into. A product that is one plain token deploys none of the vault, portfolio or pricing contracts.

The layers

Seven groups of contracts, each with one job. The tokens sit in the middle. Above them is what calls them on someone's behalf, and below them what they ask and where their money goes.

The life of a transfer

Every balance change takes the same path: a transfer, a mint, a burn, a forced transfer, an executed intent, a vault deposit or redemption. Here is a holder's plain transfer. One movement follows each step in full.

  1. The holder calls transfer(to, amount). OpenZeppelin's _update routes every ordinary transfer, mint and holder's burn into one internal function, _move. A forced transfer and an executed intent enter it through _moveAs, an enforcement mint through _mintAs, and the burner's burn calls it directly, each naming its kind.
  2. The token describes the movement. It works out the kind (here TRANSFER) and builds the context the rules will see: the sender, the receiver, the amount, the account that called, and both balances before the move.
  3. The token runs its own checks. While the token is paused, only forced movements pass. The amount must fit in the sender's unfrozen balance, the part the agent has not held back; a forced movement may take frozen tokens, up to the balance. The forced movements are a forced transfer, burn, which only a BURNER_ROLE holder can call, and the 7540 vault's two recoveries.
  4. The hook asks the token's rules in order. Each product has its own list. The demo profile full lists CredentialRule (the sender may leave, the receiver holds a credential the token accepts), MinPositionRule, HolderRestrictionRule (address freeze and lockup), SettlementWindowRule on the intent token (executed intents only), and FundHoldersRule (the fund's investor cap). The first rule that answers anything but TRANSFER_OK stops the movement, and the call reverts with ERC8473PolicyRejected(reason).
  5. The balances move, then afterTransfer runs. The hook tells the rules that asked for it about the finished movement, through after_. FundHoldersRule is the one shipped rule that asks: it tells Fund when the sender stops holding this class and when the receiver starts.

Three design choices

  • The policy lives outside the token. The token's code has no opinion about who, where or when. A policy change is a different rule list on the hook, not a token upgrade, so the token keeps its address, balances and history.
  • Installed code must be attested. Hooks, rules, implementations, portfolios, adapters, validators and fee models are each checked against PolicyRegistry, where the security council records the code hash of what it reviewed. A token checks its hook and each upgrade; the hook checks each rule; ProxyDeployer checks a token's first implementation; a portfolio checks its adapters; Fund checks its rule. The registry is asked at install time, so withdrawing an attestation does not change a running token.
  • Authority waits, and the fast power can only stop. Changes to a product's rule list, roles and code go through a timelock, 24 hours by default and with a floor of one hour. The guardian can pause at once and cancel queued changes, and cannot unpause, change the rules or upgrade. A second timelock can unpause and remove the guardian, so the guardian cannot block its own removal. A launch is the exception: the operations account places a product with no wait, because a launch adds a proxy over attested code and touches nothing that exists.

Glossary

token
A contract that holds balances and asks its hook before each movement. The stack has six templates: four ERC-20s, GatedToken, IntentToken, Gated4626Vault and Gated7540Vault, one ERC-721, Gated721, and one ERC-1155, Gated1155.
template
One kind of token, with its own implementation and its own templateId. An upgrade to an implementation that reports another template is refused.
mixin
A contract that adds one ability to a template. IntentSettledUpgradeable adds signed intents, and IntentToken is the template that uses it.
proxy
A small contract that keeps a token's address and storage and runs the code of another contract, the implementation, for each call. Each token is a TokenProxy (OpenZeppelin's ERC1967Proxy). The upgrade function sits in the implementation (the UUPS pattern).
implementation
The shared code of one template, deployed once per core and used by each proxy of that template. Its constructor disables setup, so a proxy is what gets set up.
product
One token an issuer launches: a proxy over a template, with its own rule list and policy.
profile
A named combination of products in ignition/profiles.ts, from one plain token (token), one NFT (nft), one multi-token (multi) or one intent token with both executors (intent) to the demo (full): an intent token and both vaults as three classes of one fund, with both executors. The demo has no desk, portfolio or fee model; managed-fund and vault4626-invest show those.
hook
The contract a token asks before each balance change. Here it is ComplianceHook, one per deployment, holding an ordered rule list for each token.
rule
A small contract that answers one policy question about a movement with a reason code, such as TRANSFER_OK or RECEIVER_FROZEN. One instance serves each token whose list names it, and any setting it keeps is kept per token.
reason code
A bytes32 answer, the hash of a name like LOCKED_UP. They are listed in contracts/interfaces/Errors.sol and on One movement.
kind
What sort of movement it is: TRANSFER (a holder's transfer), EXECUTED (a signed intent run by an executor), FORCED (an enforcement move: the agent's, the burner's burn, or on a 7540 the settler's recovery of a redemption in a closed period), MINT or BURN (a holder's own burn, such as a vault redemption). Rules decide differently per kind.
attested
The security council recorded a contract's code hash in PolicyRegistry as a reviewed HOOK, RULE, IMPLEMENTATION, PORTFOLIO, ADAPTER, VALIDATOR or FEE_MODEL. It is checked when the contract is installed. A code hash covers an immutable address, not the code at that address.
guardian
A multisig that can stop things and reduce risk, and nothing else: pause the tokens and the executors with no delay, cancel operations waiting in the main timelock, cancel a proposed price, pull a portfolio's money back, lower its caps, lower a 4626's rate limit, and revoke a guarded account's permission. On the credential registry it can block an address or switch off an attester.
timelock
A contract that holds admin powers and runs a queued operation after a delay. The main timelock administers the products and the contracts around them. The recovery timelock is the same contract with no recovery role of its own; outside itself it can unpause, remove the guardian, take away any account's right to cancel on the main timelock, and undo the guardian's actions on the credential registry, and nothing more.
operations account
The account that owns ProxyDeployer and runs product launches. It is a product's admin during its launch and hands that to the timelock at the end of it.
intent
A transfer a holder signs off-chain (an EIP-712 message): amount, receiver, deadline, a random single-use nonce, and optionally which executor may run it and what the holder is to receive in return.
executor
An account holding EXECUTOR_ROLE on an intent token, the role that runs an intent. A product names the ones it uses: IntentExecutor, DvPExecutor or both.
relayer
An account that submits someone else's signature and pays the gas.
DvP
Delivery versus payment: the shares and the cash move in the same transaction, or neither moves. DvPExecutor does this from two signed legs.
cash leg
The side of a DvP trade that pays. It is a signed intent on a gated cash token, or an ERC-3009 authorization on a token outside the stack. DvPExecutor runs it first, and the other side is the securities leg.
fund
A Fund contract: a set of share-class tokens with one investor count and one cap across them.
class
One token registered in a fund. An investor who holds three classes counts once.
credential
The record an attester (an identity provider) keeps for an address in CredentialRegistry: who vouched for it, its qualification bits, until when it is valid, and its standing. A jurisdiction is a qualification bit, not a country field.
frozen amount
The part of an account's balance that the agent holds back on a token (setFrozenTokens). The holder can move the rest. It is not the same as an address freeze, which HolderRestrictionRule keeps and which stops the address from sending or receiving.
minimum position
A per-token minimum balance kept by MinPositionRule: a transfer, intent, mint or holder's burn may not leave an account holding more than zero and less than the minimum. Forced moves, which include the burner's burns, and exempt accounts are outside it. A minimum of 0 switches it off.
escrow
Tokens a contract holds on someone's behalf. Gated7540Vault holds the shares of pending redemptions at its own address.
controller
In ERC-7540, the account a request belongs to, which may claim it or let an approved operator do so.
dealing period
On Gated7540Vault, the stretch of time whose requests are dealt together. One period is open and requests join it. Everyone in a period gets one price, set in one transaction, and each investor claims later.
cut-off
The moment a dealing period closes, with closePeriod. A request after it waits for the next period. Through NavDesk, anyone can call it once periodLength has passed.
net asset value
NAV: what a fund's assets are worth after fees, in units of its asset. settlePeriod deals a period at the NAV it is given, which leaves out what the vault itself holds (pending deposits and claimable redemption cash).
redemption gate
The most of a fund that may leave in one period, net of what comes in, set on NavDesk as a level. Past it every request in the period is filled at the same fraction, and the rest carries to the next period, stays invested and deals at that period's price.
maker and checker
Two different accounts behind one decision: one proposes and the other approves. NavDesk asks this of each price, and Distributor of each distribution.
portfolio
A Portfolio contract, one per vault: holds the vault's idle money and sends it to installed adapters, under caps on their allocation.
adapter
The plug between a portfolio and one place money can go. ERC4626Adapter holds a position in an on-chain vault, and GuardedAccount serves a custodian or a strategy.
priced value
What the contracts can read on-chain: a portfolio's idle money plus what its priced adapters say they hold (pricedValue).
reported value
A figure the vault's reporter states for money the chain cannot see, such as money with a custodian. NavDesk.propose takes it with the hash of the report behind it. The hash proves which report was used, not that the money exists.
distribution
A payment to one token's holders. Distributor holds a Merkle root of who is owed what, built off-chain at the record date, and each holder claims with a proof.

The contracts

One page per contract: what it is for, how it works, what it can do, and its tests.

Token

PolicyGatedCoreUpgradeable

What every template shares, whatever it counts: the roles, the guardian and recovery, the pause, the hook pointer and the upgrade checks.

 PolicyGatedERC20Upgradeable

The base every fungible token and vault extends: balances, frozen amounts, forced transfers, and the one path that asks the hook.

 IntentSettledUpgradeable

The mixin for transfers a holder signs and an account with EXECUTOR_ROLE runs.

 GatedToken

The base and nothing else: a gated ERC-20 at the decimals its ledger uses, with no signed intents.

 IntentToken

A gated ERC-20 with signed intents, at the decimals its ledger uses.

 Gated721

A gated NFT: a certificate or a note per token, judged by the same hook and rules, with a freeze per token.

 Gated1155

A gated multi-token: several series in one contract, each a token id with its own supply, judged by the same hook and rules, with a frozen amount per holder and id.

Vaults

GatedVaultBase

What both vaults share: the issuer's mint and burn revert, and a third party in a deposit or redemption must pass the token's policy.

 Gated4626Vault

An ERC-4626 vault whose shares are a gated token. It can invest through a portfolio, with a limit on how fast its counted value may rise.

 Gated4626Management

The 4626's second contract: plugging a portfolio, the rate limit, the fee model and paying fees run there, on the vault's storage. The implementation creates it, and nobody can swap it alone.

 Gated4626Common

The storage and the counting those two share, so they cannot disagree on the layout.

 Gated7540Vault

An ERC-7540 vault dealt in periods: investors request, one settlement prices the whole period, and each investor claims later. A settlement can take part of the redemptions, at one fraction for all.

 Gated7540Settlement

The 7540's second contract: closing, settling, rejecting and recovering run there, on the vault's storage. The implementation creates it, and nobody can swap it alone.

 Gated7540Common

The storage and the request roll those two share, so they cannot disagree on the layout.

 NavDesk

Turns a reported value into an approved price for one 7540 vault: one account proposes, a different one accepts, and a cooldown leaves time to cancel. It holds the fund's redemption gate, if it has one.

 FeeModel

Works out a management fee and a performance fee from rates fixed at deployment, with no storage of its own.

Portfolio

Portfolio

Where one vault's money may go: held idle here, sent to installed adapters under caps on their allocation, returned to the vault, paid to the fee model's RECIPIENT by a 4626's collectFees, or moved by a timelock operation to an attested successor.

 ERC4626Adapter

Holds a portfolio's position in one ERC-4626 vault as an ordinary depositor, priced on-chain.

 GuardedAccount

An adapter for a custodian or a strategy, whose value is reported. It makes a call only if a validator allows it.

 ERC20CallValidator

Allows transfer to one address, or approve of one spender, up to one amount per call.

 ERC4626CallValidator

Allows deposit, mint, withdraw and redeem when the money and shares come back to the calling account.

Policy

ComplianceHook

The one hook a deployment shares. An ordered rule list per token; the first rule to say no wins.

 BaseRule

The parent of every rule: default answers, and the guard that lets only a token's installed hook call after_.

 CredentialRule

Who may hold: reads the credential registry and applies the token's policy to a receiver. A sender needs a standing that lets it leave.

 MinPositionRule

Refuses a movement that would leave an account with more than zero and less than the token's minimum.

 HolderRestrictionRule

Per-holder lockups and address freezes, set by the token's agent.

 SettlementWindowRule

Executed intents settle inside the token's daily UTC window, on the weekdays it enables.

 FundHoldersRule

Caps the number of distinct investors across a fund's classes.

 SupplyCapRule

Refuses a mint above a per-token cap, to limit what a stolen minter key can do.

 ExecutedOnlyRule

Refuses a plain transfer, so a holder can send tokens to another account only by a signed intent. A forced transfer still passes.

Identity and fund

CredentialRegistry

One record per address: which attester vouched for it, its qualification bits, until when, and its standing.

 Fund

Lists a fund's share classes and counts distinct investors across them.

Governance and deployment

PolicyRegistry

The list of code hashes the security council has attested, and as what kind.

 GovernanceTimelock

OpenZeppelin's timelock with a one-hour floor on its delay. The main and the recovery timelock are both this contract.

 ProxyDeployer

Places each token at a predictable address, refuses an unattested implementation, and refuses to finish unless the token came up as expected.

 TokenProxy

The proxy every token is: OpenZeppelin's ERC1967Proxy, created without setup data.

Settlement and distribution

IntentExecutor

Submits signed intents that state no payment, one at a time or as an all-or-nothing batch.

 DvPExecutor

Settles shares against cash in one transaction from two signed legs.

 SignedEntry

Lets an investor enter a vault on signed messages while a relayer submits them and pays the gas.

 Distributor

Pays one token's holders a funded distribution from a Merkle root that a maker proposes and a different checker approves.
