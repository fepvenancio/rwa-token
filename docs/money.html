Overview / Assets and settlement

Where the money sits

For each way value moves, this page shows where the asset and the shares are at every step, who can move them from there, and the exact formulas behind prices and rounding.

In one minute

Two kinds of value move here. Token balances: every template is a gated ERC-20, and each balance change asks the hook and its rules first. The vault asset: the two vaults take in and pay out an ordinary ERC-20 chosen at deployment, such as a stablecoin, and those transfers never go through the hook. The intent token has no asset behind it: its balances are the record. The 4626 vault keeps its asset itself and prices shares by formula. The 7540 vault passes deposits on to a custody address and lets a trusted fulfiller name the price. The DvP executor swaps a security against cash in one transaction and keeps nothing.

Intent token: the token is the instrument

IntentToken holds no asset and moves no payment. Its balances are the record of who owns what. An intent is a transfer the holder signs off-chain and an executor submits; its ref field ties the move to an off-chain payment message.

WhoCan move a holder's tokens byLimits
The holdertransferOnly the unfrozen balance, and the rules judge both sides.
A spendertransferFrom, with an allowance set by approve or by a signed permitAs for the holder, plus the allowance.
An executor contract (EXECUTOR_ROLE)executeTransfer(intent, sig)Only with the holder's signature, once per nonce, inside its validity window, and only if the intent names this executor or none.
The agent (AGENT_ROLE)forcedTransfer(from, to, amount)Any amount up to the whole balance, frozen part included. The receiver must hold a credential the policy accepts and must not be address-frozen.
The burner (BURNER_ROLE)burn(from, amount)No allowance needed and may take frozen tokens. Refused for an address-frozen holder and while paused.

An intent can state what its signer is to receive (considerationToken, considerationAmount). The token never moves it and never checks that it was paid. It only refuses a stated consideration that names no executor (CONSIDERATION_NEEDS_EXECUTOR), so that only the named executor can run it. IntentExecutor refuses every intent that states one (IntentExecutor_ConsiderationNotSupported), because it cannot enforce a payment. DvPExecutor is the one that does, below. Gas is paid by whoever sends the transaction, which for an intent is the operator, not the holder.

Gated4626Vault: assets in, shares out

A vault takes in an asset and issues shares, a claim on part of what it holds. Gated4626Vault is OpenZeppelin's ERC-4626 vault with gated shares: it keeps the asset itself, and a deposit or a redemption happens in one call.

The price

OpenZeppelin's formulas, with a decimals offset of 6. The offset adds a million virtual shares and one virtual unit of asset, which makes the first-depositor donation attack unprofitable. totalAssets is the vault's own balance of the asset.

OpenZeppelin ERC4626Upgradeable, _decimalsOffset() = 6
shares = assets × (totalSupply + 10^6) / (totalAssets + 1)
assets = shares × (totalAssets + 1) / (totalSupply + 10^6)
totalAssets = asset.balanceOf(vault)
CallConvertsRoundsEffect of rounding
deposit(assets, receiver)assets to sharesdownthe receiver gets slightly fewer shares
mint(shares, receiver)shares to assetsupthe caller pays slightly more
withdraw(assets, receiver, owner)assets to sharesupslightly more shares are burned
redeem(shares, receiver, owner)shares to assetsdownslightly fewer assets are paid
maxWithdraw(owner)maxRedeem(owner) to assetsdownnever promises more than withdraw can deliver

Every rounding favours the vault, and the dust stays in it, so it raises the value of every share a little. The price also moves when someone sends the asset straight to the vault, and if the asset's own balance changes. There is no strategy, fee or yield code.

Example with a 6-decimal asset and an empty vault, so shares have 12 decimals. A deposit of 1,000 assets (1000e6) mints 1000e6 × 10^6 / 1 = 1e15 raw shares, which is 1,000 shares. Redeeming all of them pays 1e15 × (1000e6 + 1) / (1e15 + 10^6) = 1000e6: the 1,000 assets come back in full.

Who can move what

  • The asset in the vault leaves only through withdraw and redeem, which burn the owner's shares. The caller must be the owner or hold an allowance on its shares. No admin function moves it. An upgrade through the timelock, to reviewed code, could change that.
  • The shares move like any gated balance: the holder, a spender, and the agent's forcedTransfer. The role mint and burn always revert on the vault (Gated4626Vault_Unsupported).
  • A frozen amount cannot be redeemed: maxRedeem is the unfrozen balance.

Gated7540Vault: request, fulfil, claim

Gated7540Vault is asynchronous (ERC-7540): a holder requests, a bank with FULFILLER_ROLE fulfils later at a price it names, and the holder then claims. A request is booked under a controller, the account entitled to claim it; a controller's requests add up under one entry. The deposited asset does not stay in the vault: at fulfilment it goes to a custody address, a plain address set at initialisation that the main timelock can change with setCustody.

Where things sit, and who can move them

Redemption requestednone yetin escrow at the vault's own address, booked as pendingSharesFulfiller: fulfillRedeem burns them; rejectRedeemRequest sends them back to the controller as an ordinary transfer. Agent: recoverRedeemRequest(controller, to) moves them to to as a forced move.
Redemption fulfilledin the vault, pulled from the fulfiller, booked as claimableAssetsburnedController or its operator: claim with redeem or withdraw, to any receiver, which is not checked. Agent: recoverClaimable(controller, to) pays them to to.

The escrow is one balance at the vault's address for every controller, while the bookkeeping is per controller. The vault keeps the total booked to pending redemptions: the agent's forcedTransfer out of the vault's address may take only shares above it, such as shares sent there by mistake (Gated7540Vault_ExceedsSurplus otherwise), and the vault's address cannot be frozen. For escrowed shares the agent's tool is recoverRedeemRequest, which clears the controller's pending amount as it moves them. An operator approved with setOperator is a full delegate: it can open requests that pull the owner's assets or shares under any controller, and choose the receiver of a claim.

The vault checks people at each step: a deposit request and a deposit claim require that a mint to the controller would pass the rules, the claim's mint also judges the receiver, and a redemption claim requires that the controller could still send. The asset legs are never sent to the hook.

7540 prices and rounding

The fulfiller names both numbers of every fulfilment: how many assets and how many shares. Nothing on chain compares that price with any reference; FULFILLER_ROLE is a trusted role. Share decimals equal the asset's, with no offset. The vault records the price of the last fulfilment and emits it in Fulfilled(controller, isDeposit, price, assets, shares, fulfiller).

Recorded at each fulfilment (_recordPrice)
lastPrice = floor(assets × 10^decimals / shares)
            and 1 if that is 0
starts at 10^decimals, one asset per share
Indicative views only
convertToShares(a) = floor(a × 10^decimals / lastPrice)
convertToAssets(s) = floor(s × lastPrice / 10^decimals)
totalAssets()      = convertToAssets(totalSupply)

The views above use the last price of any controller, and the assets they describe sit in custody, so they are indicative. What a claim actually pays or mints comes from the controller's own claimable pair: each fulfilment adds its assets and shares to that pair, and each claim takes from it. A claim therefore uses the blended ratio of everything still claimable. previewDeposit, previewMint, previewRedeem and previewWithdraw always revert with Gated7540Vault_AsyncOnly, as ERC-7540 requires.

Claims: a full claim takes the whole pair exactly; a partial one rounds against the caller
deposit(assets)   shares = claimableShares                                  if assets == claimableAssets
                  shares = floor(assets × claimableShares / claimableAssets)  otherwise
mint(shares)      assets = claimableAssets                                  if shares == claimableShares
                  assets = ceil(shares × claimableAssets / claimableShares)   otherwise
redeem(shares)    assets = claimableAssets                                  if shares == claimableShares
                  assets = floor(shares × claimableAssets / claimableShares)  otherwise
withdraw(assets)  shares = claimableShares                                  if assets == claimableAssets
                  shares = ceil(assets × claimableShares / claimableAssets)   otherwise

The rounding difference stays in the controller's pair, and a claim of everything is exact, so over all its claims a controller gets exactly the fulfilled amounts and the vault keeps nothing. In raw units:

  • A deposit pair of 1,000 assets and 800 shares. deposit(333) mints floor(333 × 800 / 1000) = 266 shares and leaves 667 and 534. mint(267) then uses ceil(267 × 667 / 534) = 334 assets.
  • A redemption pair of 7 shares and 10 assets. redeem(3) pays floor(3 × 10 / 7) = 4 assets. From the same pair, withdraw(4) uses ceil(4 × 7 / 10) = 3 shares.

A zero input reverts with Gated7540Vault_ZeroAmount on all four claims, and so does a deposit that would mint 0 shares or a redeem that would pay 0 assets. Because mint and withdraw round up, they can use up one side of the pair before the other; the remainder is then delivered against 0, which does not revert, and the totals stay exact.

Delivery versus payment, with gated cash

Delivery versus payment (DvP) means the security and the cash change hands together or not at all. DvPExecutor does it for a security that carries signed intents, such as IntentToken. In this form the cash is itself a gated token with intents. Each party signs an intent on the token it gives: the seller on the security, the buyer on the cash, each naming the DvP executor and stating what it is to receive.

The legs mirror when the parties are swapped, each amount is the other leg's stated consideration, each stated consideration token is the other leg's token, and the two tokens differ (DvPExecutor_LegsDoNotMatch). Each signature covers its own leg, so the operator chooses only which signed legs to pair and when to submit. A leg that states a payment cannot run alone: IntentExecutor refuses it, and the token refuses any executor but the one it names (WRONG_EXECUTOR). The DvP executor needs EXECUTOR_ROLE on the cash token, which that token's own administrator grants.

Delivery versus payment, with ERC-3009 cash

ERC-3009 is the standard behind USDC's signed transfers. Here the buyer signs a receiveWithAuthorization payable to the DvP executor, for the price, with a nonce that ties it to this one trade: keccak256(abi.encode(securityToken, securityToken.intentHash(sellerIntent))). The cash passes through the executor inside the one call.

The authorization must come from the seller's buyer (the intent's to), for the seller's stated consideration, in the stated cash token, with the nonce above, and the two tokens must differ (DvPExecutor_LegsDoNotMatch). The security token's address is in the nonce because the executor cannot tell an honest token's intent hash from a counterfeit's. A failing pull or delivery reverts with DvPExecutor_LegFailed. A failing payout to the seller is not wrapped: it reverts with the cash token's own error, or with SafeERC20FailedOperation if the token returns false.

The pre-flights canExecuteDvPGated and canExecuteDvP3009 answer (ok, reason) without reverting: PAUSED for a paused executor, LEGS_DO_NOT_MATCH, then for ERC-3009 the authorization's window, nonce and the buyer's balance, then each leg's own canExecute.

What the code does not have

  • Fees. No contract charges or pays a fee on any flow.
  • Interest, yield, strategies or a price oracle. The 4626 price moves only with the vault's asset balance and its share supply; the 7540 price is whatever the fulfiller names.
  • ETH handling, outside the timelocks. No contract of the system defines receive or a payable function. Each token proxy has OpenZeppelin's payable fallback, but it forwards to an implementation whose only payable function is upgradeToAndCall. That refuses value without setup data (ERC1967NonPayable), and with setup data the call it makes is to a non-payable function, which reverts; the *_Ether tests pin this. OpenZeppelin's TimelockController, and so GovernanceTimelock and the recovery timelock, has receive() and a payable execute, so the two timelocks can hold ETH and send it with an operation.
  • A sweep. Nothing recovers tokens sent to a contract by mistake. The DvP executor says so in its NatSpec. The 4626 vault counts any asset sent to it as part of totalAssets, so it raises the share price. The 7540 vault pays out only what its bookkeeping records, so an asset sent to it directly has no function that moves it.
  • A cancel for requests. A 7540 requester cannot withdraw a pending request; the fulfiller can reject it, and that is why a deposit request is refused up front when the controller could not hold the shares.
  • DvP matching, partial fills or more than two legs. The parties agree a DvP trade off-chain and sign fixed amounts.

Why it is built this way

  • The asset leg is not hooked. The rules decide who may hold the gated token; the asset is an ordinary ERC-20 under its own issuer's rules. Gating the share movement is enough to gate entry and exit: a deposit is a MINT to the receiver and a redemption a BURN from the owner.
  • Asset in first, out last. The 4626 vault pulls the asset before minting and burns before paying, OpenZeppelin's order, so a rejection or a re-entering asset token finds a consistent state.
  • The 7540 price is explicit. The fulfiller sets it per fulfilment and it is published in an event; with no on-chain exchange rate to inflate, the vault needs no decimals offset. Claims use each controller's own pair, not the last price, which may come from another controller's fulfilment.
  • Rounding against the caller, exact in full. Partial claims round so the vault never pays more than it received, and a full claim settles the pair exactly, so nothing is stranded.
  • The DvP executor keeps nothing. Gated cash moves directly between the parties; ERC-3009 cash passes through within one call, and the balance checks make any leak revert the trade.
